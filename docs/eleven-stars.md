# Go Bag at 1 to 11 stars

Airbnb's 11-star exercise, applied to Go Bag on 14 Nov 2026. An earlier run of it placed
#150 at 6 stars and #149 at 7, but its ladder was not saved. This is the ladder, rebuilt
around those two, so the next brief can say where it sits and build on it.

The moment it stretches is **"Is my family ready, right now?"** Going up the ladder, three
things grow together: the household does less of the work, Go Bag is present closer to the
emergency itself, and more of the household takes part.

## The ladder

1. **A ready-made "72-hour kit"**, bought, put in a closet and forgotten. When the power goes
   out, the flashlight's batteries are dead.
2. **A generic printed checklist.** The same list for a single 25-year-old in Phoenix and a
   family of five with a baby on the Gulf Coast.
3. **A checklist app you fill in by hand.** Typing every item; most people stop at 20.
4. **Photograph a kit, get an inventory and a score.**
5. **Go Bag as of 14 Nov 2026.** Scores sized to who lives here, where, and the emergencies
   that matter; gaps named by essential; a shopping list ranked by the points each line adds,
   with Amazon picks; what is spent and what the rest costs; a family plan; weight and space
   for each bag. It is very good at the audit, and it is a tool you have to remember to open.
6. **The kit stays true over time** (#150). A supply is followed from ordered to packed to
   expiring, and replacement reminders land on the calendar.
7. **Go Bag reaches out when it matters** (#149). A winter storm watch is issued and the
   household gets an email: how ready it is for that storm, and three free things to do
   tonight.
8. **It works during the emergency.** "It's happening: wildfire" gives each kit's grab list in
   order, which bag goes in which car, the meeting place, contacts and medications, offline on
   every phone in the family, because the network is often down. A 10-minute family drill goes
   on the calendar twice a year.
9. **The kits refill themselves.** A box with exactly the household's replacements arrives
   before anything expires, already counted as packed. On the street, people know who has the
   generator and who has medical training.
10. **Help arrives already knowing the family.** Responders know about the insulin in the
    fridge, the dog and the wheelchair. Evacuation routes are picked live. The car was packed
    yesterday because Go Bag saw the forecast.
11. **A drone drops your packed bag** wherever you are standing when the earthquake hits, and a
    hotel two counties away is booked in your name.

## The sweet spot, as of 14 Nov 2026

**8 stars, cut to its smallest version.** Rungs 1 to 7 are about the time between
emergencies, where Go Bag is now strong and where two briefs already sit. Nothing yet covers
the first hour of a real emergency, the moment the product exists for. The smallest test is a
**one-page go sheet for each emergency**: what to grab, from which kit, in what order, plus the
meeting place and contacts, printed or saved to the family's phones so it works offline. #149's
option B, a printed sheet for each bag, is halfway there. It needs no new outside service and
should come from data the app already keeps.

What holds it back:

- **No evidence anyone wants it.** Every brief so far notes that nobody has been seen using a
  feature and no check-back has a verdict. So the first step is not code: a 10-minute family
  drill with the app open, noting where it fails to help. That would be the project's first
  observed evidence.
- **The page is at its size budget** (93,881 of 94,000 bytes when #150 was written). Even a
  small go sheet likely means raising the budget or finishing the split in #111.

## Using this

A brief that comes from this ladder names its rung, as #149 and #150 do. When a rung is
built, or a check-back changes what it means, update rung 5 and the sweet spot here in the
same pull request.
